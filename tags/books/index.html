Beasts of the Sea  /  Das Wesen Des Lebens  — Iida Turpeinen, FISCHER Taschenbuch

The book weaves together stories of people connected to Steller’s sea cow and its extinction, though only indirectly. The focus is on their personal fates, though only indirect. It also explores other species driven to extinction.

Prima Facie  — Suzie Miller, Kjona Verlag

I devoured this book. Told from the perspective of a defense attorney, it explores sexual offenses, critiques the legal system, and exposes the immense obstacles victims face in seeking justice, as well as the alarmingly high number of unreported cases.

Aborted after page 126, total of 395 pages. Even though I was interested in the topic of the book, somehow the story did not catch me. I am not sure if this relates to the translation or the original, but some parts had a weird writing style.

James  — Percial Everett, Hanser / Blessing

The book follows a slave in the southern USA during his escape journey. Based on Huckleberry Finn: a book I’ve never read. Hence I read it with relatively few preconceptions. Topic-wise James tackles a dark chapter of humanity, particularly of the USA, but only touches lightly on the harsh sufferings.

NSA  /  Nationales Sicherheits-Amt  — Andreas Eschbach, Lübbe

What if electric computers and the internet existed before the nazi regime and the second world war? This book begins with a glimpse what surveillance and exessive data collection can be used for in a totalitarian regime, effectively even worsening the holocaust.
